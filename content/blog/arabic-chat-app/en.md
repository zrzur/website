Most chat apps were made in English first, with Arabic added later: buttons in the wrong place, word-for-word translations, text that jumps between left and right. Zrzur is different. We built it with Arabic speakers in mind from the first day, and even its name is Arabic: the زرزور, the starling, is a bird that flies in large flocks, which is how we see communities. This post explains what it means for a chat app to be Arabic from the ground up.

## Right to left, everywhere

When you choose Arabic, the whole interface turns around: the server list, channels, messages, settings, dialogs and menus. We test this on a computer and at phone width. If you still find a screen that stays left to right, tell us and we will fix it.

## Modern Standard Arabic, not machine translation

The app has more than **six thousand** strings, all translated into Modern Standard Arabic and checked **line by line**. We write «سيرفر» for server, because that is the word people use, and we write sentences the way a person writes them. If a sentence is unclear, tell us and we will fix it.

## A clear Arabic typeface

Arabic text in Zrzur is set in **IBM Plex Sans Arabic**, a clear typeface that is easy to read for a long time, whatever interface font you pick. Your browser downloads it only when Arabic text is on screen, so people who do not read Arabic do not load it at all.

## Moderation that understands Arabic

Word filters in many apps only know English. In Zrzur, **AutoMod** offers an optional list of common **Arabic insults** to start from: switch it on and it becomes a rule you can edit. You can add your own words, use `*` to match part of a word, and keep a list of words that are always allowed. Everything about moderation is in our guide on [keeping your server safe](/blog/moderate-your-server).

## Everything a chat community needs

Arabic is not the only thing. Zrzur has what you expect from a modern chat app:

- **Servers** with text and voice channels, forums and threads.
- **Voice channels** you drop into, **screen sharing** with sound, **stage channels** for talks and events, and a **soundboard** with a library of 115 ready sounds.
- **Roles and permissions**, a **lobby** that welcomes newcomers, and **welcome questions** that give them their roles.
- **Events**, to plan a game night or a meetup in your server.
- **Discover**, to find Arabic communities and join them in a click.

More on voice in [voice chat and screen sharing](/blog/voice-chat-screen-sharing), and the steps to build your community in [setting up a community server](/blog/set-up-community-server).

## In the browser, no app store needed

Zrzur runs in any current browser on a computer or a phone, so you need no app store and no install to start. Invite links like `zrzur.cc/your-code` open in any browser too. For your computer there is an app for Windows, macOS and Linux on the [download page](/download).

## Other languages too

Arabic, English and German are the languages we check line by line, and the app offers many other languages, some of them still being translated. Zrzur picks your device's language on its own, and you can change it at any time in **Settings › Language**. Our website zrzur.com is in seven languages, Arabic among them.

## Open source, hosted in the EU

Zrzur is open source under the AGPL licence, and our servers are in Frankfurt am Main, Germany.

## Start now

[Open Zrzur](https://app.zrzur.com), choose Arabic if it is not already chosen, and create a server for your friends or your community. If you are coming from Discord, our post on [Zrzur as a Discord alternative](/blog/discord-alternative) has everything you need.
