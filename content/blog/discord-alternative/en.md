If you run a community on Discord, or you just hang out there with friends, you already know how a good chat app feels: a server for your group, channels for every topic, a voice channel you can drop into at any time. Zrzur works the same way. It is free, open source, hosted in the European Union, and built with Arabic from the ground up. This post goes through what you already know, shows how it works in Zrzur, and explains how to move your community.

## The same idea you already know

Zrzur is built around **servers**. A server is your community's home: you create it in a minute, give it a name and an icon, and invite people with a link. Inside it, **categories** and **channels** give every topic its own place. If you have used Discord, nothing here needs explaining, and that is on purpose: the way you talk with your people should not have to change.

What does change is the price. On Zrzur, the everyday things are free for everyone: talking, calling, sharing your screen and running a community. There is nothing to pay in this version, no subscription and no checkout.

## Text: channels, forums and threads

- **Text channels** keep everyday chat, announcements and shared files apart.
- **Forum channels** hold longer discussions: every post has its own replies and can be sorted by tags.
- **Threads** take a side conversation out of a busy channel without losing it.
- **Messages** carry images, files, formatting, emoji and reactions, and every server can add its **own emoji**.

## Voice, video and screen sharing

- **Voice channels** are always open: join in one click, leave when you want.
- **Screen sharing** right from the call; hover over someone who is streaming to see a preview.
- **Stage channels** for talks and events: speakers talk, listeners listen, and anyone can ask to speak.
- **Soundboard** with a ready library of more than a hundred sounds any server can add.
- **Push-to-talk and voice sensitivity**, so your microphone only sends what you mean to say.

## Running a community

This is where a chat app shows whether it is made for communities or only for small groups.

- **Roles and permissions** decide who can read, write and moderate, down to every channel.
- **A welcome page** for every server, with its icon, banner and description.
- **Interest questions**: newcomers answer a few questions on their first visit and get the matching roles straight away.
- **Rules and join requests**: new members accept your rules when they join, and you can approve members by hand.
- **AutoMod** stops blocked words, mention spam, link spam and repeated messages. It can block them, alert your team or time the sender out.
- **The audit log** shows who changed what.
- **Pause** invites and direct messages for a while if your server is under attack.
- **Server insights** show how your community grows: messages per channel, who joined and left, and how many people write each day.

## Discover, profiles and bots

- **Discover** lists public servers and bots by topic. You read a server's page before you join, and join in a click. Owners decide whether their server is listed.
- **Profiles** have a banner, a short bio and badges. **Designs** is a gallery of profile decorations and nameplates made by creators in our community.
- **Bots and apps**: developers can build them for Zrzur, and a verified bot carries a clear tag so people know who they are talking to.

## What is different about Zrzur

- **Open source.** Zrzur is open source under the AGPL licence. It started from an open-source chat project, and we redesigned every screen and added much more. The original contributors stay credited.
- **Arabic from the ground up.** The app reads properly from right to left. Arabic, English and German are checked line by line, and the app speaks many more languages.
- **Hosted in the EU.** Our servers are in Frankfurt am Main, Germany, and Zrzur is run from Austria. We do not show advertising based on profiling.
- **Your data stays yours.** Two-step sign-in, a list of your active sessions, a data download whenever you want, and an account you can delete yourself.
- **Everywhere you are.** In any current browser on a computer or a phone, and as a desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux.

## Bringing your community over

1. [Open Zrzur](https://app.zrzur.com) and create an account.
2. Create a server and set up the channels you already use: the same names work fine.
3. Add your roles and give each one its permissions. Turn on the welcome page, rules and AutoMod if you want them.
4. Create an invite link. It looks like `zrzur.cc/your-code` and works in any browser, so people do not need to install anything.
5. Post the link where your community already is, and start talking.

You do not have to move everyone at once. Many communities start with one channel or a weekly voice call and grow from there.
