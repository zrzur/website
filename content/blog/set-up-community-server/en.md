A good community server is not just a list of channels. It is a place where a newcomer understands in ten seconds what the community is about, finds the right channels, and feels welcome. This guide takes you through setting up a server on Zrzur, step by step, from the first channel to your first hundred members.

## 1. Create the server

[Open Zrzur](https://app.zrzur.com), press the plus button in the server list and create a server. Give it a **name** and an **icon**: the icon is what people see in their server list every day, so a clear picture works better than a detailed one. You can add a **banner** and a short **description** later in **Server settings › Overview**.

## 2. Plan your channels

Start small. Five good channels are better than thirty empty ones. A server can hold up to **200 channels**, grouped in **categories**:

- **Information**: an announcements channel only your team writes in, and a channel for the rules.
- **Community**: general chat, a channel to introduce yourself, and one or two channels for your main topics.
- **Voice**: one or two voice channels. Add a **stage channel** if you hold talks or events.

Use a **forum channel** for questions and longer topics: each post keeps its own replies, and tags make them easy to sort. For side conversations, people can open a **thread** right from a message.

## 3. Set up roles

**Roles** decide who can read, write and moderate. A server can have up to 200. Start with three:

- **Everyone**: read and write in the community channels.
- **Moderators**: delete messages, time out and kick, see the audit log.
- **Admins**: everything.

Then add **interest roles** that unlock topic channels, for example "Gaming", "Design" or "Arabic". You will hand them out automatically in step 5.

## 4. Build your lobby

The **lobby** is your server's welcome page. When it is on, everyone sees a Lobby row and the server **opens on it**. In **Server settings › Joining**, under Lobby design, you choose its **colours and layout**, a **welcome text**, **buttons** that lead to your most important channels, your **questions** and your **team**. Sections with nothing to show stay hidden by themselves, so you can start with just a welcome text.

## 5. Ask welcome questions

**Welcome questions** turn a crowd of strangers into members who find their place. On their first visit, newcomers answer a few questions, such as "What brings you here?", and each answer can give them a **role**. With the interest roles from step 3, people see the channels they care about from the first minute. They can skip the questions and change their answers later. A question can allow more than one answer.

## 6. Rules and who may join

Also under **Joining**:

- **Rules to accept**: write a few clear rules. New members accept them before they can write.
- **Who can join**: anyone with a link joins at once, or people send a **join request** and your team approves them.
- **Verification**: ask for a verified email, or a verified email and an account older than 5 minutes, before someone can write.

For filters against spam and an emergency pause for raids, see our guide on [keeping your server safe](/blog/moderate-your-server).

## 7. Make it yours

- Add up to **100 custom emoji** in **Server settings › Emoji**.
- Fill the **soundboard** with up to 8 sounds, your own or from a ready library of 115.
- Add **bots** that help your community, and decide what each one may do.

## 8. Invite people

Create an **invite link** from the server menu. It looks like `zrzur.cc/your-code` and opens in any browser, so nobody has to install anything to join. Post it where your people already are: a group chat, your social profiles, your website.

## 9. Get found on Discover

**Discover** lists public servers by topic, and people join in a click after reading your server's page. You ask to be listed in **Server settings › Overview**. Discover lists only servers whose owners asked to be there, and the Zrzur team checks each one first.

## 10. Watch how it grows

**Insights** shows messages per channel, who joined and who left, and how many people write each day. Use it to see which channels people love, and which ones you can merge or close.
