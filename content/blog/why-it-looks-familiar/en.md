From the day we started working on Zrzur, one question stayed with us: why should anyone have to struggle just to try a new app?

Imagine you have used Discord for years. Your friends are there, your servers are there, and you know where everything is. You know how to join a voice room, how to send a message, how to create a channel or change what members are allowed to do.

Then a new app comes along and says: come and try me.

You open it and find that everything is somewhere else. You go looking for the settings, for the channels, for the simplest things you used to do without thinking.

Honestly, why does it have to be that way?

That question led to one of the most important ideas behind Zrzur.

## Why we chose a familiar design

The first thing you may notice when you open Zrzur is that it reminds you of Discord.

That is not an accident.

We chose it on purpose, because we do not want someone who is used to Discord to feel they have to learn a new program from scratch.

If you are used to servers along the edge of the screen, channels next to them and the conversation in the middle, why should we move all of that just because we are making a different app?

And what is the point of a strange design that exists only so we can call it new?

To us, good design is not the kind that impresses people for five minutes and then wears them out. Good design is the kind you understand quickly, until you forget you are using an interface at all.

So we decided to keep a way of working that people already know, and to put our effort into the experience itself.

That does not mean we want to stay a copy of any other platform. On the contrary: we want Zrzur to have its own character and identity. We just do not want to change the easy things for the sake of change.

## So what is Zrzur?

Zrzur is a platform that brings friends and communities together in one place.

You can talk with your friends, create a server for your community, organise its channels, keep in touch by message or by voice, and share your screen when you need to.

Say you have a group of friends who play together. You can create your own server, with channels for your games and channels for everything else.

If your community is bigger, you can organise its topics, channels, roles and permissions, so everyone knows where to go and what they can do.

The idea is not complicated. We want people to open Zrzur and find their place, whether they are three friends or a community with a lot of members.

## Why we care so much about Arabic

Because we know how annoying it is to use an app in Arabic and feel that the language was added at the last minute.

Sometimes the words are translated, but the layout of the page does not fit. Or there is Arabic text with English buttons next to it, or menus that run the wrong way.

We want Arabic speakers to feel that the app was made for them too, not that they are guests in an English interface.

That is why we take care with the translation, with pages that run from right to left, and with how menus and settings are laid out.

At the same time, Zrzur is not only for Arabic speakers. We want people in many countries to use it in their own language, and to find it clear and comfortable.

## What can you do in Zrzur?

First, there are servers and channels.

Instead of every conversation running together, each topic gets its own place: a channel for news, one for discussion, one for pictures, and voice channels for your friends.

There are direct messages, sharing of files and pictures, and tools that help you keep up with your conversations.

Voice is a core part of the experience. You join a channel and talk with your friends, without starting a new call every time. And there is screen sharing, which is especially useful for games, or when you want to show someone how something works.

If you run a server, roles and permissions help you organise your community and decide who can see channels and who can manage them.

There are other things we are building and testing, such as events, tools for organising content, ways to make things your own, and a smoother way to move a community over.

But we want to be clear here: not every feature we work on is ready for everyone. Sometimes we have to switch a feature off for a while, so we can fix its problems and make sure it works as it should.

We would rather do that than let people use something that is broken.

## Why not add every feature at once?

Because, honestly, adding features is easier than keeping them stable.

We could add ten new features and write a long post about them. But what does that give you if the app starts to freeze, or a basic feature stops working?

So we have put a lot of our effort into testing, fixing bugs and making things faster.

We try features before we release them, we go through the problems that show up on computers and phones, and we try to fix the real cause of a problem instead of hiding it.

We will not claim that Zrzur is finished or free of bugs. The project is still growing, and some things still need work.

What matters to us is that every release is a step forward, not just a new number.

## Was Zrzur built from scratch?

No, and we want to be open about it.

Zrzur is built on open-source projects, among them Stoat (formerly Revolt).

We started from that foundation, and we keep developing it, changing it and adding our own work, to build the experience we want.

There is no reason to pretend we invented everything ourselves. Other developers worked before us, other projects helped us get here, and it matters to give that work its due.

At the same time, Zrzur has its own path: our own decisions, our own priorities, and our own way of developing the platform.

## Where do we want to end up?

We do not want people to use Zrzur just because it is new.

We want people to open it and feel at ease.

If you are coming from Discord, we do not want you to get lost looking for channels and settings. If this is your first app for communities, we want you to learn it without any hassle.

We want communities to find the tools they need, friends to stay in touch, and Arabic speakers to find an experience that respects their language.

Over time Zrzur will change and grow, of course. We will add things, improve things, and maybe rethink some decisions.

But there is one idea we do not want to forget:

**You do not have to change everything to make something better.**

Sometimes the best thing is to keep what people are used to, and to work on what really needs to get better.

That is exactly what we are trying to do with Zrzur.
